Chapter 13 Capstone Submission Level 4 · Architecture Challenge

Submit the Capstone

A design document and ADRs are the real submission here — not a new agent build. A small, focused code extension is included because one mechanism (a regulated-path-aware CI policy) benefits from being shown precisely, the same way Chapters 11-12 showed their own policy layers in code.

solution.py in this folder is a worked reference for the Meridian Ledger scenario this chapter's lesson builds — it is one way to extend Chapter 12's TOOL_POLICY_CI pattern for a regulated org, not the correct answer. Your own submission should design a workflow for your OWN chosen regulated scenario (it does not have to be payments/SOX — HIPAA, a government-contractor framework, or a different financial-services angle are all legitimate), with your own reasoning, your own ADRs, and your own honest gaps.

starter.py solution.py (one worked example, not the answer key) adr-template.md capstone-rubric.md

What to submit

  1. A specific business scenario: one real regulated domain, what the org builds, what's actually at stake if the agent gets it wrong.
  2. A permission-scope design covering both interactive and CI modes, built on Chapters 11-12's TOOL_POLICY/ TOOL_POLICY_CI pattern, adapted to your scenario's compliance-driven boundaries.
  3. A concrete audit-trail and attribution design — who is accountable for each agent action, and how would an auditor verify it from the artifacts your design produces.
  4. An explicit list of what still requires human review, and by whom — not just "a human," a specific role or designation if your scenario's compliance regime calls for one.
  5. At least one Chapter 5 failure mode, addressed concretely for an unattended, regulated context — not a restatement of Chapter 5's abstract description.
  6. At least 2-3 ADRs, in adr-template.md's format, for genuinely contestable decisions in your own design — see example-adrs/ for three worked examples from the Meridian Ledger scenario (structure/rigor reference, not content to copy).
  7. At least one thing your own design honestly does NOT solve.
Optional: complete starter.py's three TODOs starter.py has the regulated-path classifier, the CI-mode write check, and the CI-mode commit check removed. Filling them in and confirming your output matches solution.py's is a useful, small, concrete exercise — but it is not scored separately from the design document, and completing it is not a substitute for producing your own scenario's design.
Self-check before calling this done Score your own submission against every criterion in capstone-rubric.md, honestly. A submission landing "Does Not Meet" on even one criterion has a real, specific gap worth closing — the rubric names exactly which one.

Why This Project Is the Right Way to Close This Course

This is the synthesis of everything: the tested-before-trusted discipline from Chapter 3 onward, the failure-mode vocabulary from Chapter 5, the from-scratch agent mechanism from Chapters 7-9, and the permission/CI safety model from Chapters 11-12 — all applied, deliberately, to one coherent scenario with real stakes. If you can produce a submission like this, you can walk into a real design review at a real regulated engineering org and defend an agentic CI workflow to a real compliance auditor — which was the whole point of building toward this, one chapter at a time.
